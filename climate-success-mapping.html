CLIMATE SUCCESS MAPPING

Proven climate solutions exist. Success Mapping helps them spread and scale.

While climate risks continue to challenge communities across Uttarakhand, resourceful individuals are pioneering grassroots defenses against extreme weather. Success Mapping surfaces these locally tested solutions, verifies their effectiveness, and helps share them with other communities in need.

THE CHALLENGE

Rising temperatures, erratic rainfall and natural disasters are already reshaping Uttarakhand.

Uttarakhand's climate exposure is no longer a projection. The state recorded 1,813 landslides across the 2024 monsoon season, more than six times the 245 recorded in 2022. Between 2016 and 2023, cultivation area fell by 54%, and the Department of Horticulture recorded 95,630 hectares of horticultural land lost.

The water crisis runs alongside it. More than 12,000 natural water sources across the state have dried up over the past decade, and forest fires followed the same trajectory: the Forest Survey of India recorded 21,033 fire incidents between November 2023 and June 2024, a 74% rise on the same period the year before, burning through roughly 1,809 square kilometres of forest cover that communities depend on directly for their livelihoods.

The human consequence is visible in the state's settlement pattern. Of Uttarakhand's 16,793 census villages, 1,048 were already uninhabited at the 2011 census, and the state's own Rural Development and Migration Commission has since documented a further 734 villages depopulated and 565 more with a population decline of 50% or greater.

Sources: Uttarakhand Forest Department and Climate Trends analysis of Department of Horticulture data (cultivation and horticultural land); Center for Ecology Development and Research (natural water sources); Forest Survey of India satellite fire-count data (forest fires); Census of India 2011 and the Uttarakhand Rural Development and Migration Commission (village depopulation). Landslide count and 2022 comparison from contemporaneous reporting of state disaster management data.

WHAT SUCCESS MAPPING IS

Success Mapping identifies, documents, and scales local solutions that work.

Most climate adaptation funding moves toward external interventions, designed and delivered from outside the community carrying the risk. Success Mapping starts from the opposite premise: the solutions already exist.

Success Mapping runs on the same method behind every implementation programme Tri-Impact carries out: the Neev Cycle. Positive Deviance, Appreciative Inquiry and SEED-SCALE are the specific toolkit this programme uses inside that method. Positive Deviance is how it discovers what is already working, Appreciative Inquiry is how it keeps communities leading the process, and SEED-SCALE is the structuring framework that verifies a success and carries it from one household to a wider region. See how the Neev Cycle works →

01Positive Deviance

Identification of Local Innovations

Field teams look for practices already outperforming under real pressure, not solutions waiting to be introduced.

02SEED-SCALE

Evidence-Based Documentation

Each success is measured against equity, sustainability, scalability, interdependence and holism, through structured interviews and data.

03KoboToolbox

Digital Mapping and Visualisation

Verified successes are plotted on an interactive map, giving each one a location, an impact record and a scalability assessment.

04Appreciative Inquiry

Knowledge Dissemination

Findings move outward through workshops, online platforms and policy briefs, reaching researchers and officials.

05SEED-SCALE

Scaling Through Partnerships

Community leadership, government support and outside expertise carry proven practices beyond where they originated.

U-LEAD

A collaborative network ecosystem, not a closed research project.

Success Mapping is not a self-contained documentation exercise. It runs inside U-LEAD, Uttarakhand Leadership in Environmental Adaptation and Development, a live WhatsApp network that connects farmers, entrepreneurs, self-help groups, communities, NGOs, government agencies, banks and universities directly.

Members share success stories, exchange best practices and government scheme information, and take part in multi-stakeholder discussions about climate adaptation, ecosystem conservation, sustainable tourism and youth entrepreneurship across Uttarakhand. A farmer in one valley can see, in real time, what worked for a farmer three districts away.

FIELD SUCCESSES

Real people, real change, verified in the field.

Every documented success behind the 300+ figure has a name attached to it. A sample.

"Turning Rain into Savings"

Pankaj Belwal · Haldwani

His school faced water shortages and the high cost of buying tanker water. He installed deep recharge tanks and underground storage to collect, filter and store rainwater, eliminating the school's reliance on tankers and improving groundwater levels.

"Saving Forest, Saving Lives"

Virendra · Gairsain, Chamoli Garhwal

Forest fires and declining biodiversity threatened both the environment and the livelihoods that depend on it. He rallied the community for tree planting drives and installed fire safety zones with loudspeakers to sound alerts during emergencies.

"Fishing for a Future"

Ganga Singh Rawat · Sumerpur, Chamoli

He lost his private-sector job after COVID-19 and faced uncertainty. He took up fish farming with a Rs. 3 lakh MNREGA subsidy, raising 6,000 carp in a pond protected by netting and fencing.

"Juicing Up the Local Economy"

Gaje Singh · Chamoli Garhwal

Few income sources were available in his village, and the local economy had stagnated. He started a small business making juice and pickles from local fruit, selling directly at roadside stalls.

"Mushrooms Blooming Through Winter"

Rahul · Sadhauli, Haridwar

He had failed at mushroom farming due to erratic seasons. After ICAR training, he scaled to 22 organic huts using gunny curtains for natural climate control, leasing land to adapt to later winters.

"Harvesting Health with Polyhouses"

Harendra Shah · Mahalchori, Chamoli

He saw untapped demand for organic vegetables but faced growing climate unpredictability. He transitioned to organic farming across three polyhouses with an 80% Horticulture Department subsidy, using drip irrigation.

"Blending Traditional and Modern Technique"

Jaswant · Craftsman

Modern concrete cattle shelters were too cold in winter and too hot in summer. He built a two-storey structure: a ground floor shelter in traditional stone and mud, and a modern RCC upper floor. Cattle health and milk yield improved.

"From Farming Risk to Eco-Tourism"

Urmila Devi

Drought, unpredictable rain and wildlife damage made her farming increasingly unreliable. She shifted to less water-intensive aromatic farming and built a bamboo eco-homestay for tourists, a stable income independent of climate risk.

"Zero-Waste Construction"

Vinod Negi

He needed a guest and storage space above his poultry store, on a limited budget. He built it from jute sacks dipped in mud, hardened to function like plywood, on a steel frame. Cost: under Rs. 60,000. It has held up for four years.

WHY THIS SCALES

A documented practice is only useful if it can travel.

A method that works in one valley does not reach the next valley on its own. Success Mapping evaluates every documented practice against five criteria before it is considered ready to travel.

✓

Equity

Does participation and benefit reach across the community, not just one household.

✓

Sustainability

Does the outcome hold environmentally, economically and socially over time.

✓

Scalability

Can the practice be replicated and adapted to a different region.

✓

Interdependence

Does it depend on functioning collaboration between community, government and outside expertise, not one actor alone.

✓

Holism

Does it address environmental, social and economic dimensions together, not one in isolation.

A success that does not meet these criteria stays documented. It does not move forward for scaling.

The methodology, questionnaire design, data collection protocols and training materials built around these criteria are transferable rather than site-specific: any community-based team can be trained to apply the same identification, verification, mapping and dissemination process to a different geography carrying comparable climate pressure.

The digital infrastructure behind it is built the same way. The KoboToolbox platform underlying Success Mapping's field data collection already holds structured records covering agriculture, housing and energy successes, each mapped to a location with an impact and scalability assessment attached. Replicability is not a future ambition. It is built into the methodology, and tested against explicit criteria, from the first success documented.
